# Historical forecast evidence: source check

The selected Iowa weather feature is the average of GFS MOS `P12` forecasts initialized at 00 UTC from April 1 through May 20. The +24-hour value is the chance of at least 0.01 inch of liquid-equivalent precipitation during lead hours 12–24, not rainfall amount or probability across a full 24-hour day. See the [NOAA message format guide](https://www.weather.gov/media/mdl/mdltpb05-03.pdf), section 9.

[NWS guidance](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=model+output+statistics) describes an approximately four-hour delay between 00 UTC model cycles and MOS availability. This general timing is useful context; it does not establish when any individual historical bulletin was disseminated, nor whether the current IEM archive retains its original version.

IEM documents a [raw text archive and station-product IDs](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/mos/) but its [AFOS cleanup code](https://github.com/akrherz/iem/blob/main/scripts/dbutil/clean_afos.py) includes `MAV` products in a 7–31-day cleanup window. A direct check of [MAVAMW for April 1, 2025](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=MAVAMW&fmt=text&sdate=2025-04-01T00:00Z&edate=2025-04-02T00:00Z&limit=5&order=asc) returned `ERROR: Could not Find: MAVAMW` on November 5, 2026. This single negative result does not prove every bulletin is missing or rule out another source. A potential alternative is [NOAA's NCEI text archive request process](https://aviationweather.gov/tools/archive/); availability of these particular products has not been checked, and no request was submitted.

The separate [IEM MOS download service](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/mos.py?help=) does provide historical parsed forecast rows: spot checks for Ames in 2010 and 2025 returned `runtime`, `ftime`, and `p12` values. The saved notebook's forecast input audit contains 2,558 selected rows for all 51 station-years in 2010–2026 and reconstructs all 51 station summaries from those saved rows. The browser-selected subsets do not independently establish that every source row was selected, however; the service's current archive provides no individual publication time or revision history. IEM describes real-time processing, but this is not proof of a specific historical row's availability or immutability. The checksums verify retained records, not their age or their source's historical completeness.

An exploratory check of one [DRI eastern-area archive file for April 1, 2016, 00 UTC](https://cefa.dri.edu/Operational_Products/MOS/data/archive/160401/00EA.txt) found fire-weather summaries for named sites, but no `P12` field or identifiers `KAMW`, `KCID`, or `KSUX` in that file. It cannot corroborate the project's station precipitation probabilities as inspected. This is a check of one file, not evidence about every DRI product or date; no file was retained locally.

[NOAA NCEI's SRRS archive](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/weather-climate-models/service-records-retention) also lists raw text products from 2001 onward and offers direct HTTPS access to hourly bundles and smaller scan files. Whether these contain the particular Iowa MOS bulletins, how to extract them efficiently, and whether any associated time proves dissemination remain unverified. A bounded request for one scan file from the Kaggle session failed at DNS resolution with Internet disabled. No data were obtained and the NOAA archive must not be treated as corroboration yet.

NOAA also offers an [AIRS bulletin-selection form](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/has/HAS.StationYearSelect?applname=SRRSBTNSEL&datasetname=9957ANX&dtypesort=dtypeord&outdest=APPS&stationsort=id&subqueryby=STATION) for a bounded station, product and hour range. The form requests an email for delivery, so no order was made. A [National Weather Service identifier list](https://www.weather.gov/media/notification/tins/tin04-09gfs_mos_fin.pdf) lists North Central GFS MOS as FOUS23 KWNO / MAVNC1. Its applicability to the relevant station and year, form indexing, and retained bulletin availability still need confirmation. A returned current archive copy would be additional comparison evidence, not by itself proof of exact historical dissemination or immutability.

The ledger records the source values and retains unknown publication or vintage fields as unknown. The strict replay therefore cannot certify the historical forecast inputs. Do not replace unknown individual publication times with a generic four-hour assumption. A checksum verifies recorded content against a saved hash; it cannot prove past availability. Likewise, [FRED notes](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/releases_dates.html) that source release dates do not necessarily coincide with availability on FRED or ALFRED.
